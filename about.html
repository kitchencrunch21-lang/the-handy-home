About The Handy Home

The Handy Home started the day I got a $180 quote to fix a squeaky door. A door. It squeaked. The fix took me nine minutes with a hammer, a nail, and a dab of petroleum jelly I already owned. That is when it clicked: most small home repairs are not hard, they are just poorly explained.

This site exists to explain them well. Every guide here is written for someone who does not own a tool belt and has never touched a pipe wrench. If a step needs a tool, I tell you which one, what it costs, and what to do if you do not have it.

What counts as a "handy home" repair?

The sweet spot is the job that sits between "call a professional" and "ignore it until it gets worse." Dripping faucets, squeaky hinges, clogged drains, wobbly shelves, tired caulk, scuffed drywall. The stuff that annoys you every day but never feels urgent enough to schedule.

I do not cover rewiring, gas lines, or structural work. Those jobs belong to licensed pros, and I will say so whenever a guide gets near that line. Knowing what not to touch is part of being handy.

Who writes this?

Hi, I am Jake Morrison. I am not a contractor. I am a regular homeowner who got tired of paying service-call minimums for ten-minute jobs, so I learned to do them myself, one YouTube rabbit hole at a time.

Every guide on this site is something I have actually done in my own house. If a method failed on me, you will read about the failure too, because the wrong way teaches as much as the right one.

The house rules

  • Real tools, real costs. If a job needs a $12 tool, I say so up front. No surprise supply runs.
  • Honest difficulty ratings. A ten-minute fix is labeled a ten-minute fix. A weekend project is labeled a weekend project.
  • Safety lines are bright. When a job should go to a pro, the guide stops and tells you.
