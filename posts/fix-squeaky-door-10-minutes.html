Fix a Squeaky Door in 10 Minutes

JM Jake Morrison ·5 min read ·10 min Easy

Every house has one. The bedroom door that announces itself at midnight. The bathroom door that squeals like it is being interrogated. You have lived with the sound so long you barely hear it anymore, but your guests do, and so does everyone you wake up at 2 a.m.

The good news: a squeaky hinge is one of the simplest repairs in the entire house. The squeak is dry metal rubbing against dry metal inside the hinge knuckle. Lubricate it properly once and it goes quiet for years. Here is the whole job, start to finish.

What You Need

Almost nothing. A hammer, a nail (or a nail set, or a thin screwdriver), a rag, and a lubricant. Petroleum jelly works great and you probably own some. Silicone spray works too. Avoid standard WD-40 for this job: it is a solvent, not a lasting lubricant, and the squeak will be back in a month. White lithium grease is the pro choice if you want to buy something.

Step 1: Find the Guilty Hinge

Open and close the door slowly while pressing your ear near each hinge. It is almost always just one of them, usually the middle or top hinge on an interior door. Once you know which one, close the door and wedge something under it, a doorstop or a folded towel, so it does not shift while you work.

Step 2: Pop the Pin

Place the tip of the nail against the bottom of the hinge pin and tap it upward with the hammer. The pin will slide up out of the hinge. Pull it free with your fingers. If it is stuck, a pair of pliers with a rag wrapped around the pin will give you grip without scratching it.

Step 3: Clean and Lubricate

Wipe the pin clean with the rag. You will likely find old grease turned to black gunk, dust, and maybe paint specks. Get it all off. Then coat the pin generously with your lubricant: a thick smear of petroleum jelly or a good spray of silicone. Work it into the hinge knuckles too, top and bottom.

Step 4: Reinsert and Test

Slide the pin back down into the hinge and tap it gently until it seats. Remove your door wedge and swing the door open and closed a few times to work the lubricant through. Wipe off any excess that squeezes out. Listen. Silence.

When the Squeak Survives

Sometimes lubrication is not enough, and that tells you something useful. A pin that is bent will squeak no matter how much grease you apply. Roll the pin on a flat table: if it wobbles, it is bent. Replacement hinge pins cost a couple of dollars at any hardware store.

The other stubborn cause is a hinge that has worked loose. Grab the hinge and try to wiggle it. If the screws are loose, tighten them. If the screw holes are stripped and the screws just spin, pull one screw out, push in a wooden golf tee coated with wood glue, snap it flush, and drive the screw back in. The hinge will hold like new.

One more tip for the future: when you paint a door, mask the hinges or remove the pins first. Paint inside a hinge knuckle is a top cause of brand-new squeaks, and it is completely avoidable.
